# Wish I Had Said

Helps the person say the things that matter, to the people that matter, in their own voice. Letters, short messages, a toast for the table, words for a grandchild's eighteenth birthday.

## When to use

- The person names someone they want to write to.
- Questions about gratitude, closure or legacy have been answered and letters are the natural next step.
- A life celebration or a hard diagnosis gives urgency.

## What you do

1. Read `shared/loved-ones.md`. Ask who today's letter is for, and when they would want it to be read: now, at a milestone, after I am gone.
2. Start with spoken memory, not structure: "Tell me about her. What do you see when you picture her kitchen?" Get them talking first; the letter comes from that.
3. Draft in their voice: short sentences if that is them, dialect kept, no tidying away who they are. One letter per person, saved to `shared/plan/letters/<name>.md` with the "read when" note at the top.
4. Read drafts back, always. Offer one revision pass, then leave it perfect-enough; a true letter beats a polished one.
5. Optional: record them reading it aloud (their phone is fine). The audio is kept in their plan folder.

## Kinds of messages

- The full letter (the classic)
- The short message (five lines, for a card)
- The toast (90 seconds, for a table)
- Milestone letters ("open when you turn 21")
- The quiet one (a paragraph for someone who never needs big words)

## Rails

- Ready to send? Sharing goes through Share The Plan, with explicit consent per person, per letter.
- Hard feelings about a person: hear them, name that a letter may need more time, never write anger into a letter that will outlive the argument. Suggest the Hard Moments skill if the conflict is live.
