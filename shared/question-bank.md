# The question bank

Thirty-five gentle questions across seven areas of life. Take them at your own pace; some may matter more than others, and skipping is always allowed.

## End-of-Life Planning

### 01. Have you identified and expressed any fears you have about dying?
*eol-1*
Understanding and expressing your fears about death helps reduce anxiety and allows loved ones to provide better emotional support during difficult times.
Helpful thought: Acknowledging fears is the first step to addressing them and finding peace.

## Legal & Financial

### 02. Have you considered writing a will, and if so, have you done it?
*legal-1*
A will ensures your assets are distributed according to your wishes and prevents family disputes. Without one, state laws determine how your property is divided.
Helpful thought: A will is one of the most important legal documents you can have.

### 03. Have you appointed an executor for your will, and does this person know their role?
*legal-2*
An executor manages your estate after death, handling everything from paying debts to distributing assets. They need to understand their responsibilities beforehand.
Helpful thought: Choose someone trustworthy who lives nearby and is willing to take on this responsibility.

### 04. Have you made arrangements to manage any business or financial obligations you have after you're gone?
*legal-3*
Ongoing financial obligations need management after death. Planning prevents missed payments, penalties, and financial hardship for your estate or family.
Helpful thought: Financial obligations don't end with death - they need proper management.

## Healthcare Decisions

### 05. Do you have a living will or advance healthcare directive in place?
*healthcare-1*
These documents specify your medical treatment preferences when you cannot communicate them yourself, ensuring your wishes are respected.
Helpful thought: Advance directives give you control over your medical care even when you can't speak for yourself.

### 06. Have you discussed your end-of-life wishes with your loved ones and medical provider? What are those wishes?
*healthcare-2*
Open communication about your preferences prevents confusion and ensures everyone understands your values regarding medical care, comfort, and dignity.
Helpful thought: These conversations, though difficult, are gifts to your family and medical team.

### 07. Have you discussed with your healthcare provider about the quality of life vs. length of life treatments?
*healthcare-3*
Understanding treatment options helps you make informed decisions about aggressive interventions versus comfort care based on your personal values.
Helpful thought: Quality of life means different things to different people - define what it means to you.

### 08. Have you made clear what treatments or interventions you wish to avoid at the end of your life?
*healthcare-4*
Specifying unwanted treatments prevents unnecessary suffering and ensures medical care aligns with your values and comfort preferences.
Helpful thought: Being specific about what you don't want is as important as stating what you do want.

### 09. Do you have a 'do not resuscitate' (DNR) order in place if you wish not to be revived in case of a medical emergency?
*healthcare-5*
A DNR order ensures medical teams respect your wishes about resuscitation, preventing unwanted aggressive interventions during medical emergencies.
Helpful thought: DNR orders must be properly documented and accessible to emergency responders.

### 10. Have you discussed the option of hospice care with your loved ones and healthcare provider?
*healthcare-6*
Understanding hospice care options ensures you receive appropriate comfort-focused care when curative treatments are no longer beneficial.
Helpful thought: Hospice care focuses on comfort, dignity, and quality of life during end-stage illness.

### 11. Have you thought about where you'd prefer to spend your last days (at home, at a hospice, at a hospital)?
*healthcare-7*
Specifying your preferred location for end-of-life care helps ensure you receive care in an environment that provides comfort and aligns with your values.
Helpful thought: Your environment during final days can significantly impact your comfort and peace.

### 12. Have you expressed your wishes for end-of-life care in the event of Alzheimer's or other forms of dementia?
*healthcare-8*
Dementia-specific directives are crucial because the disease affects decision-making capacity. Early planning ensures your values guide care decisions.
Helpful thought: Dementia care decisions are complex and benefit greatly from advance planning.

### 13. What supplements or special dietary requirements do you have beyond those prescribed by a medical professional?
*healthcare-9*
Documenting your supplements, vitamins, and dietary needs helps ensure continuity of care and prevents harmful interactions with medications or treatments.
Helpful thought: Include over-the-counter supplements, vitamins, herbal remedies, and any special dietary restrictions or requirements.

## Relationships & Legacy

### 14. Do you have a plan in place for your pets or dependent loved ones for when you can no longer care for them?
*relationships-1*
Ensuring continued care for those who depend on you provides peace of mind and prevents them from being abandoned or neglected.
Helpful thought: Pets and dependents need advocates to ensure their continued wellbeing.

### 15. Are there relationships in your life that need healing or closure?
*relationships-2*
Addressing unresolved conflicts or expressing forgiveness can bring peace and prevent regrets, both for you and others involved.
Helpful thought: Healing relationships while you can is a gift to everyone involved.

### 16. Have you expressed your gratitude to those who have been an integral part of your life?
*relationships-3*
Expressing gratitude strengthens relationships, provides closure, and ensures important people know how much they've meant to you.
Helpful thought: Gratitude expressed is a precious gift that enriches both giver and receiver.

### 17. Have you talked openly about your death with your children, or young people in your life, in a way they can understand?
*relationships-4*
Age-appropriate conversations about death help children process grief, understand life's natural cycle, and feel included rather than confused or abandoned.
Helpful thought: Children benefit from honest, gentle conversations about death and dying.

### 18. Have you considered leaving a written or recorded message for your loved ones after you're gone?
*relationships-5*
Personal messages provide comfort, guidance, and connection after death, allowing your voice and wisdom to continue supporting those you love.
Helpful thought: Your words can provide comfort and guidance long after you're gone.

### 19. Have you considered creating a video or photo album that captures memories and stories from your life?
*relationships-6*
Visual and audio recordings preserve your personality, stories, and memories for future generations who may never have met you.
Helpful thought: Stories and images become treasured family heirlooms that connect generations.

### 20. Have you thought about what you want people to remember about you?
*relationships-7*
Reflecting on your desired legacy helps guide your remaining actions and provides clarity about the values and memories you want to leave behind.
Helpful thought: Your legacy is built through your actions, values, and the impact you have on others.

### 21. Have you thought about the impact of your death on the world around you (e.g., community, environment)?
*relationships-8*
Considering your broader impact helps you think about the legacy you leave beyond family and friends, including community contributions and environmental considerations.
Helpful thought: Your impact extends beyond personal relationships to your community and environment.

## Personal Fulfillment

### 22. Have you considered what independence means to you, and how you can preserve it in your final years?
*personal-1*
Defining independence helps you make decisions about care, living arrangements, and medical treatments that align with your values and maintain your dignity.
Helpful thought: Understanding your definition of independence guides important future decisions.

### 23. Are there any places you wish to visit or see one more time?
*personal-2*
Identifying meaningful places allows you to prioritize visits while you're able, creating lasting memories and achieving personal closure.
Helpful thought: Places hold memories and meaning - visiting them can provide comfort and completion.

### 25. Are there any hobbies or activities that you have always wanted to try?
*personal-4*
Pursuing new interests while you're able can bring joy, accomplishment, and fulfillment to your remaining years.
Helpful thought: It's never too late to try something new and find joy in learning.

### 26. Have you reflected on your life, your accomplishments, and what you've learned?
*personal-5*
Life reflection helps you find meaning, appreciate your journey, and identify wisdom worth sharing with others.
Helpful thought: Reflection on your life's journey can bring peace and perspective.

### 27. Have you made peace with the fact of your mortality?
*personal-6*
Accepting mortality can reduce anxiety, increase appreciation for life, and help you focus on what truly matters in your remaining time.
Helpful thought: Making peace with mortality is often key to living fully in the present.

### 28. Have you found a way to discuss your death in a manner that feels comfortable for you?
*personal-7*
Finding comfortable ways to discuss death reduces anxiety and helps you have important conversations with loved ones about your wishes and feelings.
Helpful thought: Open, comfortable discussion about death benefits both you and your loved ones.

## Final Arrangements

### 29. Are there any cultural, spiritual, or personal rituals you want to be observed towards the end of your life or after your death?
*final-1*
Specifying meaningful rituals ensures your cultural, spiritual, or personal values are honored and provides comfort to both you and your loved ones.
Helpful thought: Rituals provide meaning, comfort, and connection to your beliefs and heritage.

### 30. Have you thought about ways to commemorate your life, such as a living funeral or life celebration?
*final-2*
Planning your own celebration allows you to participate in honoring your life and ensures the event reflects your personality and values.
Helpful thought: Celebrating your life while you're present can be meaningful for everyone involved.

### 31. Have you planned your funeral or memorial service, and if so, have you communicated your plans to your loved ones?
*final-3*
Pre-planning your service ensures it reflects your wishes, reduces stress on grieving family members, and can help control costs.
Helpful thought: Planning your own service ensures it truly reflects who you are and what you valued.

### 32. Have you considered organ or body donation, and if so, have you registered and informed your family?
*final-4*
Organ and body donation can save lives and advance medical knowledge. Clear documentation and family awareness ensure your wishes are honored.
Helpful thought: Donation decisions should be clearly documented and communicated to family.

### 33. Have you considered writing your obituary, or providing input for it?
*final-5*
Writing your own obituary ensures your life story is told accurately and includes what you consider most important about your life and values.
Helpful thought: Your obituary is often the first thing people read about your life - make it meaningful.

## Digital & Personal Assets

### 34. Have you decided who will take care of your digital legacy (online accounts, social media) after you're gone?
*digital-1*
Digital accounts need management after death. Designating someone to handle them prevents accounts from remaining active indefinitely and protects your digital privacy.
Helpful thought: Digital assets are increasingly important parts of our lives and legacies.

### 35. Are there specific personal belongings that you want to gift to certain individuals?
*digital-2*
Designating meaningful items for specific people prevents family disputes and ensures sentimental objects go to those who will treasure them most.
Helpful thought: Personal belongings often hold more emotional value than monetary worth.
